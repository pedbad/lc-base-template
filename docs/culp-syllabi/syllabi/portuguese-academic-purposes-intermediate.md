# Brazilian Portuguese for Academic Purposes (LAP) Intermediate Course

This sample syllabus focuses on advanced topics for language instruction over fifteen weeks.

## EVALUATION

**Written performance: at least 3 written assignments;**

**Oral performance**: On the eighth week – the students will be required to give a short presentation about one of the topics discussed in the first seven weeks of the course.

**Final exam:** final test with grammar questions and reading and oral comprehension.

## FIRST WEEK: The world of work - I

- **Discussion theme:** professional experience and work requirements in academia; university structure in Brazil; cultural exchanges in collaborative research across different countries;
- **Role playing:** academic research group symposium;
- **Writing:** 1) how to write a curriculum vitae and a cover letter; 2) how to write a research project;
- **Grammar:** punctuation, common expressions in letters and research project; different uses of "por que"; conjunctions and linking words;
- **Homework:** writing a CV or a research project;

## SECOND WEEK: The world of work - II

- **Discussion theme:** jobs outside academia (arts, industry, business, health)
- **Vocabulary:** personality and characteristics required for different jobs; types of jobs and work conditions;
- **Listening/Film:** "Terceirizado, um trabalhador brasileiro" (https://grupodepesquisatrabalhoecapital.wordpress.com/cultura/terceirizado/)
- **Debate:** based on the film, the students would have to reflect on the main challenges faced by workers in Brazil and make comparisons with UK, Latin America, etc.
- **Writing (Homework):** critical account of the movie;
- **Grammar:** writing a review – present and past structures (perfect and imperfect).

## THIRD WEEK: Identity matters I

- **Discussion theme**: identity matters - marketing and propaganda; soap operas and popular art;
- **Vocabulary**: description of people, cultures, tradition and culture;
- **Listening/ Film**: selection of advertisement and soap operas in Brazil – racial and class stereotypes;
- **Grammar:** foreign words used in Portuguese, formal/informal language; review of future tenses (*do presente* and future do *pretérito*)
- **Activity:** How can advertisement campaigns respect identities? How can movies (even independent) reproduce stereotypes? How to talk about differences?
- **Homework:** create an advertisement for a university programme in the UK (or somewhere else) for Brazilian students;

## FOURTH WEEK: Identity matters II

- **Discussion theme:** Brazil and its relationship with Latin America, Europe, USA, Africa and Asia;
- **Vocabulary:** countries and nationalities;
- **Reading:** graffiti, children's comics, and graphic novels in Brazil;
- **Debate:** differences and similarities between the advertisements, TV and graphic novels.
- **Grammar:** conjunctions comparison, relative pronouns and complex sentences;
- **Homework:** produce the plot of simple graphic novel;

## FIFTH WEEK: Science and technology

- **Discussion theme:** science and health in Brazil
- **Vocabulary:** ailments, symptoms, treatments, different types of medicine, body parts;
- **Film (documentary):** "Políticas de saúde no Brasil: Um século de luta pelo direito à saúde" (https://vimeo.com/117389308)
- **Debate:** importance of public health and different approaches to sickness
- **Grammar:** Present continuous, prepositions and hypothesis
- **Homework:** Review grammar exercises

## SIXTH WEEK: Religion and primitivism

- **Discussion theme:** the role of different religions in cure (Christian, native Brazilian, spiritualistic, afro Brazilian);
- **Vocabulary:** superstitions and idiomatic expressions;
- **Reading:** "Religião e cura: algumas reflexões sobre a experiência religiosa das classes populares urbanas" (http://www.scielo.br/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0102-311X1993000300019)
- **Debate:** how to reconcile the opposites in public politics;
- **Grammar:** direct and indirect speech;
- **Homework:** research about different cures and religions in Brazil;

## SEVENTH WEEK: Authoritarian regimes

- **Discussion theme:** Dictatorship and politics in Brazil;
- **Vocabulary:** political systems, democratic structures, power relations;
- **Listening/ film:** "Batismo de Sangue" e/ou "O que é isso companheiro";
- **Debate:** differences and similarities between authoritarian regimes in Latin America and other places.
- **Grammar:** revision of verbs (SER/ESTAR; IR/VIR; FAZER/PODER) and past structures.
- **Activity/ Homework:** research facts, films and texts about Brazilian dictatorship;

## EIGHTH WEEK

> [!important] Assessment
> **Oral performance:** a short presentation about one of the topics discussed in the first seven weeks of the course

- Oral presentations
- Discussion and debates about the ideas presented

## NINTH WEEK: The country and the city

- **Discussion theme:** Agrarian and urban planning and Brazilian;
- **Vocabulary:** household names;
- **Listening/Film:** "Um lugar ao sol" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pOH5SWK6Mcc)
- **Grammar:** Giving and asking for directions, treatment pronouns;
- **Activity:** debate about the questions posed by the movie: violence X home;

## TENTH WEEK: Tourism and idealism

- **Discussion Theme:** Brazilian Olympics and Soccer Games
- **Vocabulary:** hotels, reservations, tourism, etc.
- **Listening/ Comedy:** Favela tour
- **Debate:** favelas – culture and international portrait
- **Grammar:** verbal regency
- **Activity:** write a travel guide

## ELEVENTH WEEK: Favelas

- **Discussion Theme:** Brazilian Olympics and Soccer Games
- **Vocabulary:** hotels, reservations, tourism, etc.
- **Listening/ Comedy:** Favela tour
- **Debate:** favelas – culture and international portrait
- **Grammar:** verbal regency
- **Homework:** review exercises about verbs and prepositions

## TWELFTH WEEK: Art in Brazil

- **Discussion Theme:** Brazilian modernism (painting, literature and music)
- **Listening:** "Construção" by Chico Buarque
- **Reading:** "Manifesto Antropofágico" by Oswald de Andrade
- **Debate:** how art in Brazil captures its social structures: irony, humor, sarcasm;
- **Grammar:** figurative language;
- **Homework:** write a poem or a short story;

## THIRTEENTH WEEK: History of Women

- **Discussion Theme:** The role of women in Brazilian society
- **Reading:** Excerpts from "História das Mulheres no Brasil" by Mary Del Priore
- **Debate:** challenges for women in the future
- **Grammar:** future and hypothetical tenses
- **Activity/ Homework:** reading short story "Amor" by Clarice Lispector

## FOURTEENTH WEEK: New genders

- **Discussion Theme:** The rise of multi-gender identities and homophobia in Brazil
- **Reading:** "Quando o Brasil vai reagir contra a homofobia?" (http://www.cartacapital.com.br/sociedade/de-quantos-mortos-precisa-o-brasil-para-reagir-contra-a-homofobia-865.html)
- **Debate**: how to reconcile different sexual identities with religion morals, education and politics in Brazil.
- **Grammar:** passive and active voice; relative clauses;
- **Activity/ Homework:** write a blog post in response to homophobia.

## FIFTEENTH WEEK

> [!important] Assessment
> **Final exam:** final test with grammar questions and reading and oral comprehension.
