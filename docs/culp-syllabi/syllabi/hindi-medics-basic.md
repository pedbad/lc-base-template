# CULP Languages for Medics: Hindi – Basic

*Last reviewed 6 November 2018*

## What will you learn?

In this course, we shall learn modern standard Hindi spoken by the average Indian from North India and used in books, newspapers, magazines, movies etc. We shall learn the Devanagari script to write Hindi. The Devanagari script is very phonetic, in that there is only one way to read and write in Devanagari what is spoken in Hindi, and vice-versa. All North and South Indian languages use the same Sanskritic alphabet as Hindi, and all Indian scripts, like Devanagari, have evolved from the Brahmi script. Thus, learning the Hindi language and the Devanagari script will prove to be a great advantage for those interested in learning other Indian languages.

## What is Hindi?

Hindi is the official language of the Central Government of India, and serves as the lingua franca for communication between people from different regions of the country. Hindi/Urdu is an Indo-Aryan language. In other words, it is a daughter of Sanskrit. Hindi and Urdu are basically the same language: the only two differences between them are that the former has a more Sanskritized lexicon, while the latter a more Persianized one, and that the former is written using the Devanagari script, while the latter using the Perso-Arabic Script. Hindi and Urdu speakers can interact with, and are able to understand each other perfectly well, and the two languages are completely mutually intelligible. Ethnologue 2017 also categorized Hindi and Urdu together as one language, and found that 'Hindustani' (a mix of Hindi and Urdu) is the third most spoken language in the world, after English and Mandarin. A substantial proportion of speakers of three of the ten most spoken languages, English, Punjabi and Bengali, live in or are from the Indian subcontinent, and can understand and speak Hindi/Urdu. Henceforth, we shall refer to Hindi/Urdu/Hindustani as 'Hindi'.

## Who speaks Hindi?

Many varieties of Hindi are spoken as first languages by people living across North India. As per the 2011 Census of India, if we put the many dialects of Hindi together (including standardized Hindi, Bhojpuri, Maithili, Urdu), roughly 50 percent of India's population speaks Hindi as their first language. Roughly another 30 percent of Indians speak a non-Hindi Indo-Aryan language as their first language - Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Oriya, Punjabi, Sindhi, Kashmiri, Nepali, Konkani, Assamese etc. These languages, by virtue of being Indo-Aryan sisters of Hindi, are syntactically (grammatically) very close to Hindi and significantly share their vocabulary with Hindi, so Hindi is widely understood and spoken among these populations as well. The Dravidian languages - Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Tulu, Kannada etc. - which are spoken by the remaining 20% or so of the Indian population are, like Hindi, enriched by Sanskrit words, and so the southern states also understand and speak Hindi to a good extent. Thanks to Bollywood, news channels and social media, Hindi is being learnt, spoken and understood throughout India including areas like the North-East and central tribal pockets which speak Sino-Tibetan and Austroasiatic-Munda languages respectively. Hindi is also spoken and understood in other South Asian countries like Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri-Lanka, Nepal and Afghanistan. Hindi is the second most spoken South Asian language in the UK and Canada after Punjabi, and most Punjabi speakers can understand Hindi. Hindi is the most spoken South Asian language in the USA. Many Mauritian, Fijian, and Caribbean people have some Indian ancestry, and so Hindi is popularly used in these countries as well.

## How will Hindi help?

Indian diaspora communities have gone on to become among the most successful populations across the world and thus Hindi continues to grow in its capacity as an international language. Hindi is also the best language to learn to understand India in its entirety. India's rich and elegant cultures are bound together by Hindi, which represents India's unity in diversity. Be it a film set in Mumbai or a political organization's headquarters in Delhi, be it a hospital in Rajasthan or a school in Assam, be it a shopping complex in Calcutta or an IT office in Bangalore, be it a restaurant in Ahmedabad or a book store in Hyderabad, Hindi is spoken just about everywhere. Hindi won't just give you access to the animated life of India's streets and markets, but also to the eclectic thought and writing that are reflected in the medieval and modern literary heritage of the nation. Different genres of poetry and prose have shaped the versatile character of the Hindi language. The bhakti movement in medieval India witnessed everyone from Kabir to Meera to Tulasidas writing devotional poetry in one variety or another of Hindi. When India struggled to win back her independence from the British, and when the subcontinent suffered from the horrors of partition, the challenges, aspirations and misery of India found their voice through passionate outpourings in Hindi. Be it Osho's profoundly philosophical works, Premchand's gripping novels or Harivansh Ray Bachchan's mesmerizing poetry, Hindi has something contemporary to offer for each and every one of us.

## Hindi Basic syllabus

- In this course which spans ten weeks, we shall meet once a week for two hours. The aim of the course is to help the student pick up the basics of conversational Hindi, and read and write simple Hindi texts. This course is meant for absolute beginners and no prior knowledge of the language is required.
- The pace of the class will depend on how quickly students are able to grasp what is taught and how much work they put in after class, and so we may or may not be able to cover all the subjects listed below. The topics and concepts presented here constitute a rough outline, and we may change or reorder the contents during the course if need be.
- The second class onwards, for every class the student attends, he/she must be well-versed with the grammar structure taught in previous classes, failing which he may not fully understand the concepts introduced in that class. While practicing the language functions, we shall use grammatical concepts taught in not only that class but also all the previous classes.

## Class 1

**Language Function**

- Pronouncing the alphabet
- The arrangement of the alphabet
- Stop Consonants and their features:
  - Nasalization
  - Voicing
  - Aspiration
- Continuants:
  - Fricatives
  - Semivowels
  - Vowels
- Writing the alphabet:
  - Consonants with and without the thematic vowel /a/
  - Vowels

**Grammar Structure**

- Deletion of thematic /a/ at syllable ending positions in spoken Hindi
- Right to left movement for this deletion

**Homework**

- Phonetics based Exercise on alphabet – arrangement of sounds using pronunciation and description of sound features (using audio files in MP3 format)
- Watch videos (links given)
