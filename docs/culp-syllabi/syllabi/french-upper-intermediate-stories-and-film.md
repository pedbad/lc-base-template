## Lesson 1: French Fairy Tale (1): Little Red Riding Hood, Charles Perrault

**Topic and narrative context**

- What is the "francophonie"
- Student experiences with legends, fairy tales, myths and folklore and films in their own language.
- About oral literature
- Classification of fairy tales: the different categories
- Structure and role of fairy tales.
- Origins and versions of Little Red Riding Hood: who eats whom
- "Little Red Riding Hood" in the oral tradition, by Yvonne Verdier through an ethnographic reading: "Façons de dire, façons de faire" ("Way of saying, way of doing").
- Details lost in translation through different versions.
- Grammar through reading and translation
- Comparative study between the Little Red Riding Hood in the Chanel No. 5 ad by Luc Besson and the one in Perrault's version.

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Common vocabulary in legends and fairy tales.
- Grammar in context: Imperfect, perfect, pluperfect, Simple past
- Focus on the imperfect and perfect

**Preparation BEFORE the lesson**

- Read/ listen to Le petit Chaperon rouge.
- Research: who is Charles Perrault
- After the lesson: Read/ Listen to Le Chat botté
- How is the typical structure of a fairy tale (see PowerPoint 1) used? Note down which events occur in each part chronologically. What is the moral?

## Lesson 2: French Fairy Tale (2): Puss in boots by Charles Perrault

**Topic and narrative context**

- Into which category does this fairy tale fit? Why?
- Similar fairy tale in different countries.
- Answers questions (Homework).
- Moral of the story, topics,
- Description and evolutions, goals of the characters.
- Two official morals: choose the one which is the more appropriate.
- Hermes and his boots.
- Boots in other fairy tales.
- The influence of this fairy tale on other stories, cinema etc.
- Activity: In small groups, students imagine one major change in the story:
  - a) The cat refuses to help.
  - b) The ogre is clever.
  - c) The king distrusts the cat.
  - d) The marquis/ the princess falls in love with someone else.
- They note how events would unfold differently.
- Each group retells the new version in French, using:
  - Si + pluperfect → past conditional (Si le chat n'avait pas menti, le meunier serait resté pauvre.)
  - Connectors (since, although, despite, provided that).

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Si + plus-que-parfait → conditionnel passé
- Simple past
- Complex connectors (as, although etc)
- Adjectives
- Adverbs
- Vocabulary: 17th century French. Differences.

**Preparation BEFORE the lesson**

- Start writing your own fairy tales using official structure, finding a moral; each part of the structure/tenses can be introduced by wording mentioned in class (PowerPoint 1)
- Watch: Le fabuleux destin d'Amélie
- Research on either:
  - Jean-Pierre Jeunet
  - Main characters and their evolution (show don't tell)
  - Setting: Montmartre, Paris as a cinematic city. Does Jeunet's vision romanticise to the point of erasure of reality? How is Paris shown compared to reality?
  - Café culture

## Lesson 3: French Film (1)/ Fairy tale: Le fabuleux destin d'Amélie Poulain (Jean-Pierre Jeunet)

**Topic and narrative context**

- French cinema
- Discovering the film through the poster of the movie and the trailer (role of colours, light and objects)
- Intertextual references in Amélie (silent film techniques, fairy-tale narrative, surrealist touches).
- Use of music (Yann Tiersen) as a narrative device.
- Discovering the characters through "their little pleasures"
- Present yourself like a character in the film (reuse vocabulary of the senses)
- Renoir's painting "Luncheon of the Boating Party" as a key element of the plot and a topic
- Scenes and their analysis:
  - a) Amélie finds the box of memories. Discussion: Why does she decide to find the owner? What does this tell us about her character? "Life fits in a small box": students imagine what they would put in their own box.
  - b. Amélie's stratagems for Nino (playful romance). Discussion: Is her indirect approach charming or childish?
  - c. The end – the scooter (resolution). Discussion: How has Amélie changed? Is the ending satisfying?
- If Amélie lived today… invent a modern version of her adventures (social media, smartphones, online dating).

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Vocabulary on cinema: how to talk about films, techniques ; how to give your opinion about a film.
- Vocabulary and expressions on solitude, joy, shyness, tenderness, obsession
- Personality adjectives: dreamy, eccentric, reserved, kind
- Vocabulary on abstract themes: destiny, chance, happiness, altruism, imagination
- Idiomatic expressions
- Proverbs
- Grammar in context:
  - Conditional
  - Past tenses: Imperfect, perfect
- Vocabulary and structures to talk about memories

**Preparation BEFORE the lesson**

- Read (On Moodle) La Belle et la Bête
- Research on: Jeanne-Marie Le Prince de Beaumont and previous version by Mme Gabrielle-Suzanne Barbot de Villeneuve. Differences?
- Jean Cocteau
- Jean Marais
- We learnt the structures of fairy tales (see PowerPoint 1): how does Beauty and the Beast follow the rules, storylines in each part… what is the moral of this fairytale?

## Lesson 4: French Fairy Tale (3): Beauty and the Beast by Jeanne-Marie Le Prince de Beaumont

**Topic and narrative context**

- Answering questions (Homework)
- The origins of the story. Its influences. (plays, ballet, cinema)
- Psyche and Cupid: Role of the mirror
- Mental map around the word "bête"
- What is the true moral of the fairy tale: appearance vs. essence or love as transformation?
- Cocteau: a film adaptation
- Gustave Doré: How Gustave Doré's Engravings influenced the Film Version
- How does the film version influence Disney's version.
- Activity: In pairs students imagine alternative plot lines, e.g.:
  - Belle refuses to return to the castle.
  - The Beast decides to remain a beast by choice.
  - The rose is destroyed.
- Use si + pluperfect → past conditional structures. Then present to the class and defend why their ending is "better" or more "realistic."
- Activity: Role-play TV talk show in which characters are interviewed after the events of the story.
  - One student is Belle, another is the Beast, others play the sisters, the father, or the enchantress.
  - The journalist asks challenging questions:
    - Do you regret your choices?
    - Did becoming a prince really make you happy?
    - Was it love or Stockholm syndrome?
- Practice of indirect speech.

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Grammar in context:
  - Demonstrative
  - Adverbs
  - Adjectives
  - Conditional
- Vocabulary: fairy tale vocabulary in 18th century France. Differences.

## Lesson 6: French Film (2)/ Les Intouchables, Olivier Nakache et Éric Toledano: A modern fairy tale?

**Topic and narrative context**

- Answers to questions prepared as homework
- Historical and Social context: social divide between a young man from the suburbs and a rich person at the point of history
- Film poster: what does it suggest?
- Social handicap vs physical handicap vs emotional handicap
- Culture shock: analysis of scenes where music/art are used, from opposition to dialogue.
- Friendship: students pick the most beautiful scenes showing the bond between Driss and Philippe: Is it realistic or too good to be true?
- Shadowing of the scene at the Art Gallery
- Debate: Does friendship have social boundaries?
  - Two groups debate:
    - a) Social and cultural differences should not influence friendship.
    - b) Inequality inevitably creates barriers.
  - Students must back up their points with examples from Philippe and Driss's evolving relationship.
- Activity: Students act out famous scenes (paragliding, job interview, birthday party) with variations:
  - a) Change of setting (different country, modern twist)
  - b) Swap personalities (Driss becomes shy, Philippe becomes extroverted)
  - Play in exaggerated formal or slang register
- Why can we say that Les Intouchables is a modern-day fairy tale?

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Comparatives, superlatives; expressing inferiority and equality.
- Imperatives
- Relative pronouns
- Pluperfect
- Vocabulary: how to express prejudices, friendships
- Formal vs slang

## Lesson 11: French film: La passion de Dodin Bouffant (Trần Anh Hùng)

**Topic and narrative context**

- Answer questions (Homework)
- What does our way of cooking and eating say about our culture and identity?
- Analysis of the poster and the different titles given to this film: Pot-au-feu vs La passion de Dodin Bouffant vs Le goût des choses. (Students choose their preferred title and justify their answer)
- The two meanings of "passion".
- Introduce the historical context: late 19th century, French haute cuisine, master/servant relationships in a context of emotional equality.
- Discuss (Part of their homework): To what extent is cooking used as a language of love in the film?
- Scene analysis: (3 scenes in total)
  - e.g. Opening scene: sequence of kitchen preparation between Eugénie and Dodin.
  - What does their interaction reveal about hierarchy and affection and gender roles? Are Eugénie and Dodin in a relationship of equals?
  - How does the staging (lighting, sound of cooking, pacing) build intimacy?
  - Which culinary details are emphasised - and why?
- Pair activities: what are the characters thinking
- Dynamic between the two main characters: find the signs that their partnership goes beyond romance: signs of mutual respect, silent emotional codes.
- Scene 2: where Juliette Binoche explains that by eating her food it is somehow as if she were sitting at the table with the guests.
- Activity: Food critics (examples of 19th century food critics articles given):
  - Students take the role of a 19th-century food critic attending one of Dodin's legendary dinners.
  - Delivery of a short-spoken review of the evening, commenting on: The menu, the atmosphere, the "philosophy" behind the meal
- Activity: The freedom of the cook:
  - Debate: Should Eugénie have accepted Dodin's marriage proposal earlier?
  - One person defends their autonomy and choice to remain independent.
  - The other argues that marriage wouldn't diminish their freedom but rather deepen their partnership.
- Director's choices. How style shapes meaning: visuals, sounds, silences, pacing.
- Traditions vs new ways of life

**Grammatical structures and vocabulary**

- Vocabulary on:
  - Gastronomy: dishes, gastronomy, refinement, local produce, recipes, simmering, plating, seasoning
  - Arts & Craft: know-how, masterpiece, precision, aesthetic
  - Relationships: complicity, devotion, restraint, desire
  - Mortality and legacy.
- Vocabulary about lighting, painting, impressionism
- Evaluative structures: It is clear that…, It cannot be denied that…, It is clear that…
- Use of subjunctive to express emotion, desire and necessity
- Participial clauses: e.g. Ayant préparé le plat…
- Hypothetical and Conditional structures
- Period-appropriate formality and rhetorical flourish of language.
