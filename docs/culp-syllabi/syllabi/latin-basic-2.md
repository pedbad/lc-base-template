# Latin Basic 2

## Description

This course provides students with a proficiency-oriented immersion in Latin with the primary goal of reading fluency. It emphasises the study of grammar (both morphology and syntax) as well as vocabulary acquisition, which are organised around extensive reading of a continuous story in graded Latin. Although the focus of this class is on reading comprehension, students will also develop skills for translation from Latin into English and try their hand at Latin composition.

Students are expected to consolidate the material covered in class by completing suggested exercises and reading at home between each class, which may require some 1-2 hours on average per class.

## Objectives

By the end of Basic Latin 2, students should have a good understanding of all essentials of Latin grammar and come out with over a thousand Latin words in their command. They will gain experience in both translating and composing Latin, attaining the skills to engage with Latin texts with more ease and accuracy.

The course aims primarily at developing reading fluency, with particular attention to:

- filling in the big-picture structure of the Latin language, understanding the role of each main part of Latin grammar
- how Latin sentences work (clause structure, case usage, word order, etc.)
- mastering Latin accidence (i.e. word endings of Latin declensions, tenses, and moods) in context
- building a vocabulary of up to 1300 (building on Basic 1) most frequent Latin words, which will be repeatedly encountered in reading and exercises.

## Course Plan

### Lesson 1

Summary of Latin Basic 1 material.

Adverbs: formation from adjectives, degrees of comparison, numerical adverbs.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 18

### Lesson 2

Imperfect active and passive (review). Irregular adjectives. Pronominal adjectives.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 19

### Lesson 3

Future tense (review). Negative imperative.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 20

### Lesson 4

Perfect tense active and passive. Neuters of the 4th declension.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 21

### Lesson 5

Supine. Verbal stems (principal parts). Ablative absolute with perfect passive participle. Relative time of participles and infinitives.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 22

### Lesson 6

Future participle. Future active infinitive. Future passive infinitive. Summary of participles and infinitives.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 23

> [!important] Assessment
> **Moodle assessment at home (15% of the final mark)**

### Lesson 7

> [!important] Assessment
> **In-class grammar test on Moodle (15% of the final mark)**

Pluperfect tense. Deponent verbs: perfect tense and infinitive.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 24

### Lesson 8

Ablative absolute: regular vs deponent verbs. Deponent verbs imperative.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 25

### Lesson 9

Gerund. Summary of 3rd declension adjectives. Negative expressions (summary).

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 26

### Lesson 10

Subjunctive mood. Present subjunctive.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 27

> [!important] Assessment
> **At-home assessment on Moodle (15% of the final mark)**

### Lesson 11

Imperfect subjunctive. Sequence of tenses. Result clauses.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 28

### Lesson 12

> [!important] Assessment
> **In-class assessment on paper (10% of the final mark)**

Purpose clauses. Indirect commands. Review of subordinate subjunctive clauses.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 28 (continued)

### Lesson 13

> [!important] Assessment
> **In-class grammar test on Moodle (15% of the final mark)**

*Cum*-clauses. More result clauses. Summary of purpose and result clauses.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapter 29

### Lesson 14

Future perfect. Gerundive. Overview of the perfect and pluperfect subjunctive. Sequence of tenses (summary). Conditional sentences.

**Reading:** *Familia Romana*, chapters 30-33 (excerpts).

### Lesson 15

> [!important] Assessment
> **Final test in class (30% of the final mark)**

Introduction to Latin verse.

**Reading:** a selection of Latin verse and prose.
